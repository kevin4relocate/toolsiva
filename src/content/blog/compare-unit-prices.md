The package with the lower sticker price is not necessarily the better value. To compare two packages of the same kind, divide the price by the quantity using a consistent unit.

Use the [Unit Price Calculator](/tools/calculator/unit-price-calculator/) when shopping for packages of different sizes.

## Example: $3 for 300 g or $4 for 500 g?

The first package costs **$3 / 300 g = $0.010 per gram**. The second costs **$4 / 500 g = $0.008 per gram**. The $4 package costs more in total, but it is cheaper per gram.

To express the same comparison per 100 grams, multiply both unit prices by 100: **$1.00 per 100 g** versus **$0.80 per 100 g**.

The comparison works because both quantities use grams. Comparing $/gram with $/kilogram without conversion can reverse the apparent result.

## Use the same definition of quantity

Before entering values, check whether the label refers to net weight, drained weight, volume or number of pieces. Two products can have different package contents even when their labels look similar.

If quantities are in different units, convert them first with a suitable tool such as the [Weight Converter](/tools/converter/weight-converter/) or [Volume Converter](/tools/converter/volume-converter/).

## Why the lowest unit price is not always the right purchase

A larger package may be wasteful if some of the contents expire before use. Storage space, quality, shipping costs and minimum purchase requirements can matter more than a small price-per-unit saving.

Use unit price as one piece of the decision, not the only one.

## Check the calculation

1. Enter two prices and two positive quantities.
2. Confirm the units are compatible.
3. Read the per-unit values.
4. Check that the result makes sense by multiplying unit price back by quantity.

A quantity of zero would require dividing by zero and is not valid input. Negative prices are also not meaningful in this shopping comparison.

A consistent denominator makes package comparisons understandable and reduces errors caused by different package sizes.
