A running pace answers a practical question: **how much time did each kilometre or mile take on average?** Speed answers the same performance question in a different unit. Pace is often easier to use while running; speed may be easier to compare with a treadmill or cycling display.

Use the [Toolsiva Pace Calculator](/tools/calculator/pace-calculator/) to convert an elapsed duration and distance into **minutes per kilometre or mile** and the corresponding average speed.

## The basic pace calculation

For any continuous activity measured by total elapsed time:

`Pace per kilometre = total elapsed minutes ÷ distance in kilometres`

A runner who completes **5 km in 30 minutes** has an average pace of:

`30 ÷ 5 = 6 minutes per km`

This is usually written **6:00 min/km**. It means the average time for one kilometre is six minutes, not that every kilometre was necessarily run at exactly that pace.

To find average speed instead:

`Speed = distance ÷ hours`

Thirty minutes is half an hour, so `5 ÷ 0.5 = 10 km/h`.

| Measurement | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Distance | 5 km |
| Elapsed time | 0 hours, 30 minutes |
| Average pace | **6:00 min/km** |
| Average speed | **10 km/h** |

## Convert a pace into race-time expectations

Suppose your target is **5:30 min/km**. That is five minutes and thirty seconds, or **5.5 minutes** per kilometre.

For a steady 10 km effort, multiply by 10:

`5.5 × 10 = 55 minutes`

A 10K at exactly 5:30 min/km would therefore take **55:00**. This is only a mathematical projection. Course hills, weather, traffic, fatigue, water stops and changes of pace may affect the actual elapsed time.

If your watch displays **moving time** instead of **elapsed time**, decide which figure you want to analyze. The Toolsiva calculator uses whatever duration you enter—it does not automatically subtract stops.

## Why pace uses a clock format

A pace of 4.5 minutes per kilometre should not be displayed as **4:50**. Half of a minute is **30 seconds**, so the correct clock-style pace is **4:30**.

To convert a fractional minute:

1. Keep the whole minutes.
2. Multiply the decimal fraction by 60 to find seconds.
3. Round seconds appropriately.

For example, `4.75 minutes = 4 minutes + 0.75 × 60 seconds = 4:45`. Toolsiva rounds pace at the second level, so a value close to the next whole minute displays naturally as `5:00`, not `4:60`.

## Kilometres and miles are not interchangeable

A **6:00 min/km** pace is not **6:00 min/mi**. A mile is longer than a kilometre, so running one mile at the same speed takes more time.

Choose `km` or `mi` in the tool to match your distance. If your treadmill, fitness app or route plan uses a different speed unit, the [Speed Converter](/tools/converter/speed-converter/) can help compare them. Converting units is separate from estimating fitness.

## Common practical questions

**Can I enter 90 minutes as minutes?** The tool expects **Hours** and **Minutes** separately. Enter 1 hour and 30 minutes, not 90 in the minutes field.

**Does it calculate split times?** It provides average pace from total time and distance. It does not compute individual kilometre-by-kilometre splits from GPS data.

**Does a faster pace have a larger number?** No. A lower **min/km** value means faster running, because each kilometre takes less time.

For planning, use the calculated pace as a reference and adjust for your actual route and ability. A mathematical average is useful, but no single target pace fits every session.
