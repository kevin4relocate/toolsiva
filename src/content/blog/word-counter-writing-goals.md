Word-count limits appear in essay assignments, application forms, articles and video scripts. Before editing toward a limit, make sure you know what the limit actually counts.

The [Word Counter](/tools/text/word-counter/) offers a quick view of text length while you work on a draft.

## Words and characters are different measurements

The phrase “A quick example” contains three words. It contains more characters because each letter and space is counted separately. A request for 500 words is therefore not interchangeable with a 500-character field limit.

Hyphenation, punctuation, emoji and languages without spaces can complicate automatic counting. Where an institution publishes its own counting rules, follow those rules rather than expecting every counter to produce identical numbers.

## Review a draft before shortening it

Paste the version that will actually be submitted. If the text exceeds its limit, look first for repeated explanations, duplicated claims and examples that do not add information. Cutting words at random can make writing less clear.

If the draft is too short, identify what a reader still needs to know. Maybe a term is undefined, a process has no example or an important assumption is missing. Adding meaningful detail is more useful than stretching sentences.

## A short pre-submission checklist

1. Identify whether the rule concerns words, characters, pages or lines.
2. Count the final edited draft rather than an earlier version.
3. Determine whether headings, captions and references count.
4. Read the result for clarity and factual accuracy.
5. If needed, check the final text in the destination system.

For a strict character field, use the [Character Counter](/tools/text/character-counter/) instead.

## Word count is not a quality score

A 300-word answer may be sufficient for a narrow question, while a complex tutorial may require more detail. Search visibility, accessibility and reader satisfaction cannot be predicted from length alone.

A counter helps you satisfy a constraint. Strong writing still depends on useful information, sensible structure and the accuracy of what you say.
